Start here

The habit: Mask → Ask → Check

Use these three steps every time you use Copilot for work. Every exercise in this workbook follows them.

1 · Mask

Before you paste anything, remove or replace sensitive data. Pricing, CUI, and health information never go in.

2 · Ask

Write the prompt with Goal, Context, Source, and Expectations.

3 · Check

Read the output as if you will sign it, because you will.

Ask: the four parts of a good prompt

PartAnswers the questionExample
GoalWhat do I want?Write a status update on the Building B-214 cooling problem.
ContextWhy, and for whom?It's for the COR, who wants to know whether classes will be disrupted next week.
SourceWhat should it use?Use only the technician notes below.
ExpectationsWhat should the result look like?Under 150 words, three short paragraphs: what happened, what we're doing, what's next.

Follow-up moves

  • Shorten: "Cut this to half the length. Keep the dates."
  • Re-aim: "Rewrite this for the technicians instead of the COR."
  • Reshape: "Put this in a table with columns Date, Problem, Action, Result."
  • Challenge: "What's missing or unclear? What would a skeptical reader ask?"
  • Show your work: "Which parts of your answer came from my notes, and which did you add?"

Check: before you use any output

  • Every fact, number, date, and name matches the source.
  • Nothing was added that I didn't provide, such as response times, phone numbers, or job titles.
  • It follows TIYA policy and doesn't commit TIYA to anything I can't commit to.
  • The tone fits the reader.
  • I put back any masked details, outside of Copilot.
  • I would sign my name to it.

E115 min

Two prompts, one task

Run the weak prompt first. Then open a new chat and run the GCSE prompt. Compare the two.

Weak prompt
Write a notice about a water shutoff.
GCSE prompt
Goal: Write a notice to building occupants about a planned water shutoff.
Context: Building B-214 (training classrooms and offices) will have no water on Thursday, October 29, from 7:00 to 11:00 AM while we replace a leaking main valve. Restrooms and drinking fountains will be unavailable. The nearest open restrooms are in Building B-216, next door.
Source: Use only the details above. Don't add any details I haven't given you.
Expectations: Under 120 words. A clear subject line. The key facts in the first two sentences. Plain language, friendly but direct.

Compare: Which could you send with fewer edits? What did the second prompt add?

Day 1 · 10:00 – 10:45

Responsible use

These rules are placeholders until your instructor replaces them with TIYA's written policy. If they ever disagree, TIYA's policy wins. When unsure, ask before you paste.

The data traffic light

Red · never enter
  • CUI, or anything you suspect is CUI
  • Health information about a person (PHI)
  • SSNs, DoD ID numbers, dates of birth
  • Pricing: TIYA rates, costs, bids, proposals, vendor quotes
  • Security details: access codes, alarm or camera information
  • Passwords and account details
Amber · mask first
  • Names, emails, phone numbers
  • Employee performance or conduct details
  • Vendor and subcontractor names
  • Contract and task order numbers
  • Internal emails and meeting notes
Green · okay to use
  • General how-to questions
  • Public information, such as published FAR clauses and regulations
  • Your own writing with amber items masked
  • Generic procedures and checklists
  • The fictional samples in this workbook

How to mask

ReplaceWith
A person's nameTheir role: [Technician A], [the COR], [Building Manager]
Phone, email[PHONE], [EMAIL], or delete
Contract number[CONTRACT]
Vendor name[Vendor 1]
Dollar amountsDelete. If you need a comparison, say "about 10% higher" only if that is itself allowed.
Health detailsDelete entirely. "On light duty" at most, and only if needed.
Access codes, security detailsDelete entirely.

Keep your key (which placeholder means what) on paper or in your own document, never in Copilot. Put the real details back after you check the output.

Day 1 · 1:15 – 2:45

SOP lifecycle lab

Take one outdated SOP through four stages: Diagnose, Rewrite, Visualize, Adapt. Copilot drafts. You own accuracy. TIYA's doc-control process approves.

Sample C · SOP FM-07, Emergency Work Orders, Rev 3, September 2019

Emergency calls should be taken by the desk and a work order should be put in. After hours the phone goes to whoever is on call, the on-call list is on the board in the shop. The tech should get there as soon as possible. If the tech can't be reached try someone else.

Emergencies are things like leaks, no AC in summer, power out, etc. Use judgment.

The system should be shut down if needed and the area should be made safe. Supervisor needs to know about it. If parts are needed, a request should be submitted through the normal process.

Call the old after-hours line 706-555-0199 if the desk phone is not working (fax 706-555-0198).

When finished the work order is closed out. Keep paperwork in the binder.

Step 1 · Diagnose15 min
Goal: Critique this SOP.
Context: It's a facilities maintenance procedure for emergency work orders on a military installation, last revised in 2019. We're updating it.
Expectations: A table: Issue | Why it matters | Suggested fix. Cover missing roles, vague wording, missing steps, safety risks, outdated items, and record-keeping. Rank the issues by risk.

[paste Sample C]
Step 2 · Rewrite20 min
Rewrite the SOP using this template: Purpose, Scope, Responsibilities, Definitions, Procedure (numbered steps, each starting with who does it), Records, References, Revision History.

Rules:
- Active voice. Every step names a role.
- Where the original doesn't give a specific value (a response time, a phone number, a job title), write [VERIFY: what's needed] instead of inventing one.
- Add a step for notifying the COR, marked [VERIFY].

Count your [VERIFY] markers: ______ . Each one is a question for a subject-matter expert.

Step 3 · Visualize15 min
Turn the procedure into:
1. A step table: Step | Who | Action | Decision point (yes/no) | Next step.
2. A simple text flowchart using arrows, with the decision points as yes/no branches.

Optional: copy the step table into Word, then use Insert › SmartArt to make a visual flow.

Step 4 · Adapt15 min

Pick two.

A. Make a one-page quick-reference card for technicians to keep in the truck: the 5–7 steps that matter most at 2 AM.
B. Write 5 multiple-choice questions to check that technicians understood the new procedure, with an answer key.
C. Write an email announcing the new procedure to technicians: what changed, why, and when it takes effect. Under 150 words.
D. Write a 3-minute toolbox talk script introducing the new procedure.

Day 1 · 3:00 – 4:30

Leadership scenarios

Use Copilot as a thinking partner. You make the decisions. For anything about people, describe the situation and never name the person.

L1 · 20 min

Announce a change

You're moving your technicians from four 10-hour days to five 8-hour days for a 90-day pilot, to get better coverage on Fridays. Some people will be unhappy.

Goal: Help me announce this change.
Context: [describe the change and your team]
Expectations:
1. A message to the team, under 200 words, honest about the trade-offs.
2. The 8 questions they're most likely to ask, with suggested answers.
3. The 2 questions I should take back to my manager instead of answering myself.
L2 · 20 min

Prepare a hard conversation

A strong, well-liked technician skipped a lockout/tagout step last week. No one was hurt. You need to address it.

Help me plan a conversation with a team member. Situation: [describe without names]. I want them to understand how serious this was and keep their motivation.
Give me: an opening line, three key points, likely reactions and how to respond, and how to close with a clear next step.
Now play the employee. Respond realistically, including some defensiveness. Wait for my reply each time.

(After three or four exchanges:)
Pause the role-play. Give me feedback on how I handled it: what worked and what to change.
L3 · 15 min

Decide between options

Your Routine backlog has 9 work orders older than 30 days. Options: authorize overtime for two weekends, bring in a subcontractor, or re-sequence work and accept a slower clear-down.

Help me think through a decision. Don't make it for me.
Situation: [describe]. Options: [list]. Constraints: [staffing, contract, timing]. Don't use any pricing; describe cost as higher or lower.
1. A table comparing the options on speed, cost, risk, team impact, and customer impact.
2. A pre-mortem: for each option, imagine it's 60 days later and it failed. Why?
3. The questions I need answered before I decide.
L4 · 20 min

Lead AI adoption

You find out a team member pasted a vendor quote into a free AI app on their phone to compare prices. They were trying to be efficient.

1. Help me plan a short, non-punitive conversation with this person that makes the rule clear and keeps them willing to use approved AI tools.
2. Draft five team norms for using AI at work: short, specific, positive, each one sentence. Base them on Mask, Ask, Check.
