FM-SOP-03 · Work Order Priority Definitions

SAMPLE · FICTIONAL · FOR TRAINING ONLY. Not a TIYA Services document.
SOP number FM-SOP-03
Revision 2 · effective March 1, 2025
Owner Facilities Maintenance Manager
Approved by Program Manager

1. Purpose

Define the four work order priorities and the response and completion targets for each, so every request is handled consistently.

2. Scope

All facilities maintenance work orders received by the Work Order Desk, by phone, email, or the work order system.

3. Responsibilities

  • Work Order Desk Coordinator: assigns the initial priority when the work order is entered.
  • Facilities Lead: confirms or changes the priority within 1 hour for Emergency and within 1 business day for all others.
  • Facilities Maintenance Manager: approves any priority downgrade of an Emergency work order.

4. Priority definitions and targets

Priority Definition Respond within Complete within
Emergency Immediate threat to life, safety, health, or government property, or loss of a critical mission function. Examples: no heat or cooling in an occupied training facility outside the 65–80°F range, active water leak, electrical hazard, sewage backup. 2 hours, 24/7 24 hours, or make safe within 24 hours and re-prioritize the remaining work
Urgent Condition that will become an Emergency or seriously disrupt operations if not fixed soon. Examples: one of two restrooms out of service, a breaker tripping repeatedly. 1 business day 3 calendar days
Routine Normal repairs with no immediate impact on safety or mission. 5 business days 30 calendar days
Scheduled Preventive maintenance and planned work from the PM schedule (FM-SOP-15). Not applicable By the scheduled date

5. Procedure

  1. The coordinator enters the work order and assigns a priority using the table above.
  2. If unsure between two priorities, assign the higher one and flag it for the Facilities Lead.
  3. The Facilities Lead confirms or changes the priority within the times in Section 3 and records the reason for any change in the work order notes.
  4. A work order that misses its completion target stays open with its original priority and is reported as overdue on the weekly backlog report.
  5. Three or more work orders for the same equipment within 60 days are reported to the Facilities Maintenance Manager as a repeat failure for root-cause review.

6. Records

Priority, any changes, and the reasons are kept in the work order system for the life of the contract.

7. Revision history

Rev Date Change
1 Jan 2022 Initial release
2 Mar 2025 Added repeat-failure rule (5.5); clarified temperature range

FM-SOP-07 · Emergency Work Order Response

SAMPLE · FICTIONAL · FOR TRAINING ONLY. Not a TIYA Services document.
SOP number FM-SOP-07
Revision 4 · effective June 1, 2026
Owner Facilities Maintenance Manager
Approved by Program Manager

1. Purpose

Make sure every Emergency work order (as defined in FM-SOP-03) gets a qualified response within 2 hours, at any time, and that the right people are told.

2. Scope

All Emergency work orders, during and after duty hours (duty hours are 0700–1600, Monday–Friday, excluding federal holidays).

3. Responsibilities

  • Work Order Desk Coordinator (duty hours) or On-Call Lead (after hours): receives the call, opens the work order, dispatches.
  • On-Call Technician: responds on site within 2 hours and makes the condition safe.
  • Facilities Lead: notifies the COR and tracks the work order to completion.
  • Facilities Maintenance Manager: notified of every after-hours Emergency by 0800 the next business day.

4. Definitions

  • Make safe: actions that remove the immediate hazard (isolate power, shut off water, cordon off the area) even if the full repair takes longer.
  • COR: the government Contracting Officer's Representative for the facilities contract.

5. Procedure

  1. Receive. The caller reaches the Emergency Work Order Desk at 706-555-0100 (24/7; after hours this forwards to the On-Call Lead).
  2. Confirm Emergency. Check the request against the Emergency definition in FM-SOP-03. If it does not meet it, assign the correct priority and tell the caller what to expect.
  3. Open the work order with priority Emergency, the building, the location in the building, and a callback number.
  4. Dispatch the On-Call Technician by phone. If no answer within 10 minutes, call the backup technician on the on-call roster.
  5. Respond and make safe within 2 hours of the call. The technician updates the work order on arrival and when the area is safe.
  6. Notify the COR. During duty hours, the Facilities Lead notifies the COR within 1 hour of the work order being opened. After hours, the On-Call Lead notifies the COR by email the same night and by phone at 0700.
  7. Repair or re-prioritize. Complete the repair within 24 hours, or, once safe, re-prioritize the remaining work under FM-SOP-03 with the Facilities Lead's approval.
  8. Close out. Record what was done, parts, labor hours, and the root cause if known.

6. Records

Work order history, on-call call log, and COR notification emails, kept for the life of the contract.

7. Revision history

Rev Date Change
3 Sep 2019 Previous version
4 Jun 2026 Rewrote to standard template; added COR notification times, backup dispatch, make-safe definition

FM-SOP-15 · Preventive Maintenance Scheduling

SAMPLE · FICTIONAL · FOR TRAINING ONLY. Not a TIYA Services document.
SOP number FM-SOP-15
Revision 1 · effective October 1, 2024
Owner Facilities Maintenance Manager

1. Purpose

Keep equipment reliable by scheduling and completing preventive maintenance (PM) on a fixed cycle.

2. PM frequencies

Equipment Frequency Typical tasks
Rooftop units (RTUs) and split systems Quarterly Filters, belts, coil cleaning, refrigerant check
Chillers and boilers Monthly inspection; annual service Log readings, check controls, annual teardown inspection
Emergency lighting and exit signs Monthly 30-second test; annual 90-minute test Test, replace lamps and batteries
Roof drains and gutters Quarterly and after major storms Clear debris
Backflow preventers Annual Certified test

3. Procedure

  1. The PM schedule for the next quarter is published by the 15th of the last month of the current quarter.
  2. PM work orders are generated with priority Scheduled (FM-SOP-03).
  3. A PM that cannot be done on its date is rescheduled within 14 days; the reason is recorded.
  4. Deficiencies found during PM that need repair become new work orders with their own priority.

4. Records

Completed PM checklists are attached to the work order.

ADM-SOP-02 · Overtime and Timekeeping

SAMPLE · FICTIONAL · FOR TRAINING ONLY. Not a TIYA Services document.
SOP number ADM-SOP-02
Revision 3 · effective January 5, 2026
Owner Business Manager

1. Purpose

Make sure hours are recorded accurately and overtime is approved before it is worked.

2. Timekeeping

  1. Employees record time daily in the timekeeping system, by work order number where one exists.
  2. Timesheets are submitted by 1200 Monday for the previous week.
  3. Supervisors approve timesheets by 1700 Monday.
  4. Corrections after approval require a correction form signed by the employee and supervisor.

3. Overtime

  1. All overtime must be approved in advance by the employee's supervisor. There is no amount of overtime that can be worked without approval.
  2. Exception: overtime during an Emergency response (FM-SOP-07) may be worked without advance approval. The supervisor approves it by the next business day.
  3. Requests for more than 10 overtime hours in a week for one employee also need the Business Manager's approval.

4. Records

Approved overtime requests are kept with the timesheet.
